PART 2: LIBRARY RESEARCH

Databases & Reference Works

Speaker: Ánh Thư
  • The Abstract Trap: Abstracts are merely summaries written by third parties. Never cite on abstracts alone!
  • Academic Databases: Unlock peer-reviewed full articles via ProQuest, JSTOR, or Google Scholar.
  • Reference Works: Encyclopedias for depth, yearbooks for figures, biographical aids for people.
⚠️ THE GOLDEN RULE

Always Consult the Full Article

Skimming summaries produces shallow fluff. Real speeches cite primary, verified full texts.

PART 3: SEARCHING THE INTERNET

Search Smarter, Not Harder

Speaker: Dương Quỳnh (DQ)
  • Step 1: Avoid broad queries like "social media" (millions of messy results).
  • Step 2: Use targeted queries: "effects of social media on student concentration".
  • Step 3: Filter by precise sources: News for recency, Books for in-depth theory.
Specific Keywords = Clean Results

PART 3: EVALUATION CRITERIA

Authorship, Sponsorship, Recency

Speaker: Yến Khoa
  • Authorship: Is the writer clearly stated? Are they qualified experts?
  • Sponsorship: Does the organization have a bias, or do they hold proven credibility?
  • Recency: Always inspect copyright or update dates. Never cite stale statistics!

Evaluation Checklist

✓ Clearly identified expert author
✓ Reputable & non-partisan sponsor
✓ Up-to-date publication timestamp
PART 4: RESEARCH INTERVIEW

Three Stages of Effective Interviewing

Speakers: Như Mộng & H’Thảo
  • Before: Define clear purpose, choose right experts, secure consent to record, prep questions.
  • During: Arrive promptly, restate objective, listen closely, take careful manual notes.
  • After: Review immediately, transcribe in standardized format, organize accurate quotes.

PART 5: NOTE-TAKING EFFICIENCY

"Messy Notes = Fluffy Speeches"

Speaker: Thế Thuận
  • 1. Over-note: Don't trust fleeting memory. If there's 5% chance you need it, write it down.
  • 2. 3-Part Format: Topic Tag + Abbreviated Source + The Fact.
  • 3. One Idea = One Note: Keep facts like Lego blocks to rearrange effortlessly.
  • 4. Draw the Line: Slap quotes on verbatim words immediately to kill accidental plagiarism.
Four Principles for More Effective Note-Taking
PART 5: CRITICAL THINKING

Think About Materials As You Research

Speaker: Phi Vân
  • Not Just Assembly: Research isn't just copy-pasting what already aligns with your opinion.
  • Stay Open-Minded: Allow new evidence to challenge, expand, or pivot your initial perspective.
  • Insight Over Routine: Critical reflection is what transforms raw data into intellectual impact.

“Research is not just about finding info to support what we already believe. It's about being open to challenge and evolve our thinking.”
